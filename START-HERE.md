# Turn your ticket work into a portfolio entry

Use your instructor’s existing Windows setup guide to create your own portfolio repository. These files follow the shared ITC3 support-portfolio structure. Keep the internal planning guide and source records outside the repository.

1. Start with the monitor upgrade or keyboard case. Open its file in `write-ups` and preview the Markdown.
2. Replace the bracketed prompts with about 100–200 words total. Explain the problem, what you found, why you chose your checks, your contribution, and the result.
3. For team work, name your own contribution and credit help. Keep the network-lab entry labeled in progress until the relevant work is completed and verified.
4. Compare your account with your notes and instructor feedback. Use observations you can explain; do not turn requested tasks or suggested wording into completed work.
5. Remove the draft note and unused prompts when the entry is finished. Keep only the skills your explanation supports, and update its README row.

Use descriptive titles and filenames. Leave out internal ticket numbers and links, requester names, asset identifiers, credentials, and raw ticket exports. A diagram redrawn for the portfolio can be useful after it is checked for accuracy and private details.

Only include completed entries in the final Selected work list. An explicitly labeled in-progress project can also be useful. Remove unused draft rows and files. Keep the README draft notice until you have finished reviewing the listed entries.

Your Network+ pass can appear in your introduction. Add an exam date or credential link only when you have the correct details.
